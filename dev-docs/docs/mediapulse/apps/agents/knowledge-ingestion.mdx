## Purpose

The knowledge-ingestion agent sends each analyzed article to the MediaPulse knowledge graph in Organon. Organon's Ingestion model reads the article against the Concepts and adds what it states: the parties and how they relate, events, corporate actions, figures and regulations, each tied by an article point to the sentence it came from and the tickers the article was placed for. A point (`mp:ArticlePoint`) holds the quote, the article it is from (`mp:inArticle`), the item it supports (`mp:about`) and the tickers (`mp:forTicker`).

The tickers come from the article, not from the model. The agent sends the listed company of each placed ticker as `mp:placedFor` on the article's Data source. The Concepts derive every point's `mp:forTicker` from `mp:inArticle` followed by `mp:placedFor`, and keep the model from writing it. A derived ticker is an inferred answer, so a reader sees it only when it queries with inference. Before 2.1.0 the model chose each point's ticker from the header, and it often picked a company whose name contains the code, such as Aman Agrindo for AMAN.

Every ministry, state agency, parliament and local government is read as a Regulator (`mp:Regulator`). Since 2.1.0 the agent no longer asks for Government body, whose records from before then stay as they are.

Only analyzed data goes, and only for tickers someone reads. An article is sent once article-analysis has placed it in a section for at least one ticker with an active subscription: an enabled subscription from an enabled user, the rule delivery uses. Its header names only those tickers. Articles that were never analyzed, articles analysis rejected, and placements for tickers without subscribers are never sent.

The contract it follows is the Organon developer page **How to ingest an article** (`how-to-ingest-articles`) in the MediaPulse project.

Before 2.0.0 the agent read each issuer's articles with its own model and wrote the per-issuer knowledge base in the `knowledge_*` tables. It no longer writes them. The tables, their endpoints and the dashboard pages stay readable until they are removed. See [Knowledge Base](/mediapulse/knowledge-base/knowledge-base).

## How it works

1. **Reads candidates** from agent-data-api `GET /organon-ingestion/candidates`: placed articles collected since `sendFrom`, first placed within `lookbackDays`, one per canonical URL with every ticker it is placed for. An article already sent for all of its tickers is left out, so a placement for a new ticker brings it back.
2. **Fetches missing bodies** with the shared fetch (`fetchSourceBodies` in `@workspace/agent-ingestion`), the same provider chain, quality gate, dead-URL cache and host breaker content generation uses. Fetched bodies are saved back to the article and each attempt is recorded as a fetch event with reason `knowledge_ingestion`. An article on an excluded host, or whose body still can't be fetched, is not sent. It stays a candidate until its first placement is older than `lookbackDays`.
3. **Looks up the listed companies** of every ticker the candidates are placed for, by code, in one SPARQL query per 100 tickers. A ticker the graph has no listed company for is left out of the articles' `mp:placedFor` and listed under `unknownTickers`. When the lookup itself fails, the run fetches and sends nothing and fails, so no article is read without its tickers.
4. **Sends each article** as one Organon ingestion job (`POST /api/v1/ingestion`), built by `buildIngestionRequest` in `@mediapulse/knowledge-graph`. Its publisher is found by registrable domain, or created, once per run. When that lookup fails, the article is sent without a publisher. Each send is recorded with `POST /organon-ingestion/sends`: the tickers it was sent for, the job id and the time.
5. **Sweeps failed jobs.** It lists the key's failed jobs (up to three pages), claims them with `POST /organon-ingestion/retries`, and retries the claimed ones. A job is claimed only while it is the article's latest job and was never retried, so each failure is retried once.
6. **Checks the evidence** of the articles sent in the `evidenceCheck.lookbackHours` before the run started, read with `GET /organon-ingestion/sends`. With a nightly run, each article is checked once, the night after it was sent. The check only reports. It never changes Organon or Mediapulse.

It never waits for a job. Organon queues it, retries temporary errors and replaces older jobs when the same article is sent again.

## Evidence check

Before the first article, the agent reads the market relations from the Concepts once: every property under `mp:relatedTo`, with its inverse. For each article sent in the window, it reads the job, its status and the links the job wrote. A job still queued or running is skipped as `unfinished`, a failed or cancelled job as `failed`, and a job Organon no longer has (finished jobs are deleted after 30 days) as `jobNotFound`. For a completed job it runs two SPARQL queries on the article's Data source, found by canonical URL:

- its points, each with the tickers it is filed under, its quote, its about item, and the relation and other party it states (inferred answers, like `article-evidence.rq` on the developer page)
- the figures the article added, each with its currency and whether a point is about it, found through the article's versions in the history graph (`?provenance=true`)

When the job wrote a market relation, a third query asks which of its parties are listed companies.

It counts six problems:

| Problem | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `quotesNotInText` | A quote isn't in the title, snippet and body Mediapulse sent, after whitespace and case are folded (`spanIsInText` in the agent). The model paraphrased instead of copying. |
| `pointsMissingTicker` | A point is filed under fewer tickers than the article was sent for. Since 2.1.0 the tickers are derived from the article, so this points at a ticker the graph has no listed company for, or at a point read before 2.1.0. |
| `figuresWithoutPoint` | A figure the article added has no point tying it to a sentence. |
| `withoutPoints` | A completed job left the article with no points at all. |
| `listedCompanyValuesWithoutPoint` | The job wrote a market relation to or from a listed company, such as an owner or a competitor, and no point in the article states that relation, or its inverse, between the same two parties. Each example has the `company`, the `relation` and the other party as `value`, all as IRIs. Values Organon rejected, such as curated profile fields, aren't counted. |
| `figuresInOtherCurrency` | A figure the article added is in a currency other than IDR or USD. That is often a sign the article is about a company abroad with a similar name or code. Each example has the figure's label and the currency code. |

When Mediapulse no longer has the article's body, its quotes aren't checked and it is counted under `quotesUnchecked`. When the job's result can't be read, its listed-company values aren't checked and it is counted under `valuesUnchecked`; the other checks still run. An error on one article is counted under `errors` and the check moves on.

## Hermes config (`config` JSON)

`sendFrom` is the only field the schema requires, but the stored config must also spell out every field whose default is a Hermes variable. Hermes fills `{{…}}` placeholders only in the config it stores. A default the agent applies itself reaches it as the literal text, so a config without `organonUrl` calls a URL that isn't one and every send fails.

```json
{
  "config": {
    "organonUrl": "{{ORGANON_API_URL}}",
    "organonApiKey": "{{ORGANON_INGESTION_API_KEY}}",
    "sendFrom": "2026-11-16T00:00:00+07:00",
    "fetch": {
      "providers": [
        { "provider": "serper", "apiKey": "{{SERPER_API_KEY}}" },
        { "provider": "diffbot", "apiKey": "{{DIFFBOT_API_KEY}}" }
      ]
    }
  }
}
```

The other fields can be left out, because their defaults are plain values.

| Field | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sendFrom` | required | The cutover. Only articles collected at or after it are sent. |
| `organonUrl` | `{{ORGANON_API_URL}}` | Organon's base URL. |
| `organonApiKey` | `{{ORGANON_INGESTION_API_KEY}}` | A write key used only for ingestion. Jobs are visible only to the key that started them, so the sweep needs the same key. |
| `lookbackDays` | 7 | How far back a first placement may be. |
| `maxSendsPerRun` | 200 | Most articles sent in a run. Each send costs a model call. |
| `maxFetchesPerRun` | 200 | Most missing bodies fetched in a run. |
| `concurrency` | 2 | Articles sent at the same time. |
| `requestTimeoutMs` | 30000 | Timeout for each Organon call. |
| `fetch.providers` | Serper, then Diffbot | Fetch chain for missing bodies, from `{{SERPER_API_KEY}}` and `{{DIFFBOT_API_KEY}}`. |
| `evidenceCheck.enabled` | true | Run the evidence check after sending. |
| `evidenceCheck.lookbackHours` | 24 | How far back before the run a send may be for its article to be checked. |
| `evidenceCheck.maxArticles` | 200 | Most articles checked in a run. Each one is a job read and two SPARQL queries, plus one when the job wrote a market relation. |
| `dryRun` | false | Read the candidates and report them, without fetching, sending or recording anything. |

The input is `{}`, or `{ "limit": n }` to send fewer than `maxSendsPerRun` in one run.

## Run result

`details` reports `candidates`, `fetchAttempted`, `fetched`, `excludedHost`, `noBody`, `sent`, `failed`, `publishersCreated`, `publisherFailures`, `unknownTickers` (the placed tickers the graph has no listed company for), the `sweep` counts (`failedJobs`, `claimed`, `retried`, `notRetryable`, or `error`), `evidence`, and up to ten `failures` with the canonical URL and the error.

`evidence` is `null` when the check is turned off, `{ "error": "..." }` when it couldn't run, and otherwise has `sends`, `checked`, the `skipped` counts, `quotesUnchecked`, `valuesUnchecked`, and each problem as a `count` with up to five `examples`.

The run fails when the listed company lookup fails, with only `candidates`, `sent` and `tickerLookupError` in `details`, or when there were articles to send and every send failed. Problems the evidence check finds never fail it.

## Cost and capacity

A read costs about $0.009 with the Ingestion model Organon uses today, plus a fetch for a missing body. Organon reads two jobs at a time across the whole server, so keep `maxSendsPerRun` near its default until the real nightly counts are known.
